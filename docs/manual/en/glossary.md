# Glossary

**Active code** — A code chosen in the codes panel while no text is selected. Its definition shows at the foot of the panel, and you can show only its codings in the document. See [The codes panel](03-coding.md#codes-panel).

**Attribute** — A variable that describes every case: age, role, neighbourhood. Each has a type (text, number, date, yes/no or choice). See [Attributes](06-cases-and-attributes.md#attributes).

**Audit trail** — The append-only record of every change to a project: who did what, and when. See [The audit trail](10-privacy-and-security.md#audit-trail).

**Backup** — An encrypted `.trama` file of a whole project, written automatically to a folder you choose. See [Backups](10-privacy-and-security.md#backups).

**Case** — A unit of analysis, usually a participant; also a site, a household or an organisation. A document belongs to at most one case. See [Cases](06-cases-and-attributes.md#cases).

**Category** — A code that only groups other codes and cannot be applied to text.

**Clipboard** — Where the computer keeps what you copy, until you copy something else. Other programs can read it.

**Co-location** — A filter for coded segments that overlap with, lie within or contain segments of other codes. See [Filters](05-retrieval-and-memos.md#filters).

**Co-occurrence** — Two codes applied to overlapping, nearby or same-paragraph passages. See [Co-occurrence](07-analysis.md#cooccurrence).

**Code** — A short label for an idea, applied to passages of text. Codes form a hierarchy in the codebook.

**Codebook** — All the codes of a project, arranged as a tree, with their colours and definitions. See [The codebook](04-codebook.md).

**Coded segment** — One passage coded with one code by one coder. Several codes on the same passage are several coded segments.

**Coder** — Someone who codes in a project. Every coded segment, memo and audit-trail entry is signed by its coder. See [Coders](08-teamwork-and-agreement.md#coders).

**Coding** — The act of applying codes to passages; also used for one coded segment.

**Cohen’s kappa (κ)** — An agreement statistic that corrects percent agreement for agreement expected by chance. See [How the statistics are calculated](08-teamwork-and-agreement.md#statistics).

**Command palette** — The search box opened with `Ctrl+K` that finds any screen, document, project or action.

**Document** — One piece of material: a transcript, field notes, a report, a set of survey answers.

**Document group** — A set of documents that belong together, such as one wave or one site. A document can be in several groups. See [Document groups](02-documents.md#groups).

**Favourite code** — A code with a shortcut key from 1 to 9, applied to selected text by pressing the key.

**In-vivo code** — A code named after the participant’s own words. See [In-vivo codes](03-coding.md#in-vivo).

**Inter-coder agreement** — How similarly two coders coded the same material. See [Comparing two coders](08-teamwork-and-agreement.md#compare).

**Jaccard index** — A measure of co-occurrence from 0 to 1 that accounts for how often each code is used.

**Keyword in context (KWIC)** — A list of search hits, each shown with the words around it, aligned on the hit. See [Text search](07-analysis.md#search).

**Krippendorff’s alpha (α)** — An agreement statistic like kappa, with chance estimated from both coders’ labels pooled together.

**Library** — All your projects on one computer, unlocked by one passphrase. See [Getting started](01-getting-started.md).

**Memo** — Your analytic writing, about the project, a document, a code, a coded segment or a case. See [Memos](05-retrieval-and-memos.md#memos).

**Merge** — Bringing a teammate’s copy of a project into yours (see [Merging](08-teamwork-and-agreement.md#merge)); also joining two codes into one (see [Merging and splitting codes](04-codebook.md#merge-split)).

**Passphrase** — The words you type to unlock your library. At least 10 characters; a few unrelated words work best.

**Project** — The material and analysis of one study: documents, codebook, coded segments, memos, cases.

**Pseudonymisation** — Replacing names and identifying details consistently across documents. See [Pseudonymisation](10-privacy-and-security.md#pseudonymisation).

**Pseudonymisation key** — The list of what replaced what. Personal data: it stays in the encrypted project and is left out of exports unless you include it.

**Recovery phrase** — Twelve words that open your library if you forget your passphrase. Keep them on paper. See [Write down your recovery phrase](01-getting-started.md#recovery-phrase).

**REFI-QDA** — The open standard for exchanging projects (`.qdpx`) and codebooks (`.qdc`) between qualitative analysis programs. See [Exchanging projects](09-exchange.md).

**Stop words** — Very common words (*the*, *and*, *of*) left out of word frequencies.

**Time stamp** — A point in a transcript linked to a moment in its recording, used to play the recording from a passage.

**`.trama` file** — Trama’s own file for one whole project, a ZIP archive in open formats, optionally encrypted with a password. See [The .trama file](09-exchange.md#trama-file).

**Transcript** — A document of speech, one speaker turn per paragraph, optionally with a recording and time stamps. See [Transcripts and recordings](02-documents.md#transcripts).
