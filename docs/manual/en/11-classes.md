# Recording and studying classes

Trama is built for research interviews, but the same tools work for studying. Record a class, have it transcribed on your computer, then mark the parts that matter and gather them by topic when you revise. Recordings and transcripts stay on your laptop, encrypted; nothing is sent to a transcription service.

This chapter puts together what is explained in more detail in [Record a call](02-documents.md#record), [Transcribe a recording](02-documents.md#transcribe), [Coding](03-coding.md) and [Coded segments and memos](05-retrieval-and-memos.md).

<a id="permission"></a>

## Before you record

- **Ask the lecturer, and check your university’s rules.** Many universities allow recording only with permission, or only for your own study. Trama asks you to confirm that everyone agreed before it records: tick that only when it is true.
- **Your classmates are recorded too** when they ask questions or speak. Keep recordings and transcripts to yourself, and do not share them without permission.
- **Keep only what you need.** Everything stays encrypted in your project, but a recording is still personal data. Once you have checked a transcript, you can remove its recording (choose **Remove the recording** in the document’s header); the transcript and its time stamps stay.

<a id="online-classes"></a>

## Online classes

For a class held over Zoom, Teams, Meet or a video platform:

1. Choose **Record a call** (the microphone button above the document list).
2. Keep your own name in the first field, and type the lecturer’s name in **The other side, in the transcript**.
3. Wear headphones, so your microphone does not pick up the lecture a second time.
4. Tick the consent box and choose **Start recording**. Trama records what your computer plays, so it does not matter whether your microphone is muted in the call.

A **recorded lecture you are allowed to download** (an `.mp4` video or an audio file) is even simpler: import the file, and Trama transcribes it after the import.

<a id="in-the-room"></a>

## Classes in the room

In a lecture room, Trama records through your laptop’s microphone.

- **Sit near the front,** close to the lecturer or to a loudspeaker. The farther away and the more echo, the more words the transcript misses.
- **Type the lecturer’s name in “Your name in the transcript”.** Trama is made for calls: everything your microphone hears is labelled with that first name. A mode made for recording in a room is planned.
- **Trama cannot tell voices in a room apart.** Classmates’ questions appear in the same paragraphs as the lecturer.
- A two-hour class records comfortably on battery; transcribing it afterwards works the processor hard, so plug the laptop in for that.

<a id="organise"></a>

## Organising a course

- **One project per course** (**New project** in the project list), or one per term.
- **One document per class.** Give each recording a name you will recognise later, such as “Week 3 — Social movements”, and gather classes with **document groups** (by module, by lecturer). For notes you write yourself, use **New empty document** in **Import**.
- **Codes as topics.** Create a code for each theme of the course or each exam question, and code the passages that explain it. Give your most used codes a number key from 1 to 9 and coding takes one key press (see [Coding from the keyboard](03-coding.md#keyboard)).
- **Memos for your own thinking:** on a passage (what the lecturer meant, a question to ask next time), on a class, or on a topic.
- **Search every class at once** with **Text search** in **Analysis**: every place a term came up, in context.
- **A revision sheet for each topic:** in **Coded segments**, choose the topic’s code and **Export** the passages to a Word document.

<a id="practicalities"></a>

## Practicalities

- **Disk space.** Recordings take about 230 MB per hour, so a term of weekly classes adds up to several gigabytes. Check that you have room, and remove recordings you no longer need once their transcript is checked. Automatic backups copy recordings too.
- **Time.** Transcription takes roughly half to the whole length of the class and runs in the background while you work. Trama does not lock itself while it transcribes, and if you close it first, it carries on the next time you open the project.
- **Accuracy.** Read the transcript against the recording before you rely on it: names, technical terms, numbers, formulas and quiet speech are often misheard. Click a paragraph’s time stamp to play the recording from there.
- **Language.** Choose the language spoken when you start recording, or let Trama detect it. It understands Portuguese, Spanish, English and about a hundred other languages.
