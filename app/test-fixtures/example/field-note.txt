Field notes — open day

Saturday 14 June, Mulberry Vale open day. Arrived 10:15. Sunny, light wind; about sixty people over the morning by my count.

The surplus table by the gate was the busiest spot. Helen and two volunteers weighed and bagged produce; people paid into a tin or not at all. A man in a hi-vis jacket took three courgettes and came back later with a bag of apples from his own tree. Nobody remarked on it; exchanges like this seem routine here.

Children from the primary school ran a “bug hotel” stall. An older plot holder (Margaret, I think) sat with them for over an hour sorting pine cones and teaching them the names of beetles. Several parents stood back and chatted among themselves; the children seemed to bridge groups that otherwise did not mix.

Around 11:30, a disagreement at the compost bays: one plot holder had dumped bindweed roots, which the compost rota forbids. Raised voices, briefly. Helen stepped in and the two men ended up turning the heap together. Worth following up: how much of the coordinator’s day goes on mediating?

The council’s parks officer visited for twenty minutes, took photos for the newsletter and left before the talk on water harvesting. Several people noticed, and one said so loudly.

Quiet corner by the pond: three people sitting apart, not talking, apparently content. One told me she comes “to hear nothing”. Dragonflies over the water, a wren in the hedge. The garden seems to offer both company and solitude, and people move between the two freely.

Lunch was a shared table under the apple tree, with dishes people had brought: lentil soup, potato salad, mint tea from the Northbank group plot (Amina had come across town with two friends). The conversation mixed plot holders from different gardens, which apparently happens rarely. Two of the older men discussed the waiting list at length and agreed it was “a scandal”, though not on what should be done about it.

Reflexive note: I was offered tea four times and a bag of broad beans. My role is fuzzy here; people treat me as a new volunteer. I need to remind people what I am doing, especially when they share personal stories.
